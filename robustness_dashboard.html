Key Finding
The MXN reverse carry hedge genuinely reduces risk — lower VaR, lower drawdowns, and lower loss probability — but the in-sample Sharpe of 0.864 was partially overfit. Out-of-sample performance is solid (0.737) though below JPY Only's exceptional 2021–2025 run. The hedge pays off most during stress periods.

Interpretation
Weights are not stable — JPY ranges 30pp, MXN 45pp, CNY the full 0–50% range. This means the 70/30 split from in-sample optimization shouldn't be treated as a fixed allocation. The optimal hedge ratio varies meaningfully with market conditions. A simpler approach like fixed 60/40 JPY/MXN might be more robust.

Why Cornish-Fisher matters
The optimal portfolio has skewness of -0.737 and excess kurtosis of 4.80. The Gaussian model estimates 99% VaR at 0.96%, but Cornish-Fisher (which adjusts for these fat tails) estimates 1.57% — 64% higher. For carry trades, always use tail-aware risk measures.

Bootstrap vs Parametric
The bootstrap produces wider tails — 99% VaR is 10.74% vs 9.29% parametric. This confirms that a Gaussian assumption underestimates carry trade tail risk by ~15%. The bootstrap is the most realistic model since it preserves actual historical dependence structures.

Read it this way
With JPY Only, there's roughly a 1-in-40 chance of losing 10%+ in a year. With the optimal portfolio, it's about 1-in-70 (bootstrap) to 1-in-108 (regime-switching). The probability of a 15%+ loss drops from 0.55% to 0.24% — less than half. Catastrophic losses (>20%) are essentially eliminated with the hedge.
Conclusions
Key Takeaways
What works

Risk reduction is genuine. Every test — historical, parametric, bootstrap, regime-switching — shows the hedged portfolio has lower VaR, CVaR, drawdowns, and loss probability than JPY Only.

The cost is modest. You sacrifice roughly 0.9% annual return for 20–25% lower tail risk metrics. For most risk-adjusted frameworks, this is a favorable tradeoff.

MXN reverse carry provides real diversification. It's not just adding noise — it has structurally different risk factors (oil, US nearshoring, LatAm monetary policy) from JPY carry.

What to watch

The in-sample Sharpe was overfit. 0.864 in-sample drops to 0.737 out-of-sample — a 15% haircut. Always discount optimized backtests.

Optimal weights are unstable. JPY weight ranged 40–74%, MXN 0–45% across re-optimization periods. A fixed allocation is simpler and potentially more robust.

JPY Only dominated 2021–2025. The hedge underperformed during the BOJ/Fed divergence era. In strong trending carry environments, the hedge is a drag.
